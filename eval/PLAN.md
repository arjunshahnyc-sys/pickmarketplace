## 2. Code changes in detail

**1. Target's internal API and public claims.** `searchTarget` (src/lib/scrapers.ts:306) calls redsky.target.com, Target's own website API, with a browser user agent. It returned a bot challenge on 50 of 50 queries and on a production check, and Target's terms (updated 2026-09-10) grant a noncommercial license only and bar automated agents and misrepresenting who you are. Remove the call and the TARGET_API_KEY. Target stays in results through Google Shopping. Then fix the copy that describes data sources: compliance (says Pick queries "Target's public catalog API"), FAQ (lists Amazon and Costco as covered; Amazon appears in 0 of 1,747 listings and Costco in 1 of 50 queries), about ("find the lowest price"), category page descriptions ("prices from Amazon", "never overpay"), how-it-works, help, TrustedBy, the homepage footnote and loading text. Every change removes a claim; none adds one.

**2. A real price on top.** In the default sort, when a verified store carries the exact item, the top slot and the "Same item" lowest-price marker go to a verified store. A listing from a seller Pick does not verify, priced under 60% of what verified stores charge for the same item, never leads, and the flag Pick already computes (`priceFlag`, never shown today) becomes a visible note such as "Priced far below verified stores". Listings that are refurbished, used, open box, resale, lease or e-textbook never rank above new units of the same item. Possible scam listings sink in every sort, including "Item price: Low to High" (src/lib/sortResults.ts:14 has no guard), and never feed a reference price (a Shein listing set The Ordinary's). Existing labels stay as they are.

**3. Condition on cards.** Pick's feed has no condition field, so detect it from the title and the kind of store (the harness rules in eval/lib/rules.ts are a tested starting point) and show it: Refurbished, Open box, Used, Resale store, Lease payment, eTextbook. A marketplace listing with no stated condition shows "Condition not stated". A verified store's listing with no condition words shows nothing until a structured source (item 10) can confirm "New".

**4. Offers dropped before ranking.** The dedup key (src/lib/searchService.ts:92) is the title alone, so a second store with the same title disappears; key it by store and title. The relevance filter (src/lib/scrapers.ts:114) needs half the query's words in the title with no synonyms; also accept a matching model number, and common equivalents (ANC, tee for t-shirt, refrigerator for fridge, 4th gen for 4).

**5. Twin gates.** Today a twin needs 25% name overlap, a price at or under 75% of the reference, 4.0+ stars with 50+ reviews and a verified store (src/lib/productGrouping.ts:549), with no spec or type check. Add: same product type (Pick's own taxonomy), not a kit, mini, sample or multipack, not a hand or body version of a face product, not the searched model in another colorway, and cheaper than Pick's own top price for the exact item. Fewer twins, all comparable.

**6. Same-item matching.** A listing with no model number currently joins any numbered model (src/lib/productGrouping.ts:207), which is how WH-1000XM5 headphones joined the WF-1000XM5 earbuds. Require model numbers to agree when either side has one and the other names the same series; require model-family words (Pro, Plus, Max, Ultra, FE, Mini, XL, Lite, Open, Sport) and gender words to agree; require the same product type. Compute the item's reference price from new single units at verified stores only. The pinned fixtures in src/lib/__tests__ will need updating; they encode today's decisions.

**7. Real stores competing.** Recognize, through the usual manual registry review: Dash, La Roche-Posay, Paula's Choice, Supergoop!, levoit.com, Hy-Vee, T.J.Maxx, Focus Camera, BuyDig, Connection and bkstr.com, then add their published shipping policies where they publish one. Decision for you: today a verified store whose shipping Pick does not know always ranks below every complete total (converse.com $35 sits at #19 under Journeys $45.78). The proposal ranks it by what Pick knows, item price plus any known shipping, with a "shipping not included" note, keeping the rule that only a complete total wins the top slot when a complete total is cheaper.

**8. Freshness.** Each card says "Price verified <date>" (src/components/ProductCard.tsx:375), but nothing is verified with the store: it is when Pick fetched Google's copy. Show "Price checked <time>" instead. Sources with their own timestamps (Best Buy's priceUpdateDate) can show those.

**9. Twin candidates.** For 25 of the 35 no-twin queries, nothing in the results was cheap enough, well reviewed and from a verified store, because twins come only from the same search. Option A: a second query built from the item's product type and key specs. Option B: a curated table of twins for the most-searched products, reviewed by you, which also makes good landing pages. Option C (after item 10): spec-based gates from Best Buy's API data. Note that Walmart's affiliate terms ban "dupe" comparisons if Pick joins that program.

**11. Per unit and variants.** Parse pack counts and sizes, show a per-unit price on packs, keep packs out of single-unit statistics, and mark "size not stated" when the search names a size the title omits.

## 3. Data sources and their terms

Checked on primary sources on 2026-10-03 by four research passes; the clauses that change the plan (Walmart's adjacency and "dupe" bans, Best Buy's 72-hour cache, logo, first-tier and written-permission clauses, eBay's co-mingling ban) were re-read on the source pages. Terms change: re-check before signing anything.

**Two findings that shape everything below**

1. **A retailer's affiliate terms govern how its prices may be shown, whatever the data source.** Walmart allows prices only from Walmart. Target allows prices only from its own datafeed. Best Buy's Publisher Agreement (section 7) bars showing individual prices, images or descriptions without its written permission, and requires sub-affiliate networks such as Skimlinks to hold their publishers to the same terms. So joining a retailer's program means switching that retailer's prices to its own data, and turning on Skimlinks for Best Buy, Target or Walmart links while showing their Google Shopping prices needs their permission first. Decide this before filing the Skimlinks application (Amazon is already excluded from tagging; the same switch can exclude these).
2. **There is no first-party alternative to Serper.** Google's Merchant API covers only a merchant's own products, the Custom Search JSON API is closed to new customers, ends on 2027-01-01 and returns no Shopping results, and Google's comparison-shopping program runs only in Europe. Serper fetches Google results on Pick's behalf; Google's terms and robots.txt disallow automated access to /search and /shopping, and Google sued a similar provider (SerpApi) in December 2025. Serper is not a retailer website, so this is not the scraping your rule covers, but it is the main dependency risk. Official sources reduce it; Sovrn Commerce (below) is the closest licensed substitute.
