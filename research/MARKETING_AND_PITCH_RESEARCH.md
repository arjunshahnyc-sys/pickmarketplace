# Pitching & Marketing Pick Marketplace — Research Paper

*A plain-English look at how other shopping apps and marketplaces got people's attention, and creative ways Pick can stand out from Amazon, Shopify, Honey, and the rest.*

---

## 1. The Big Idea First: What Makes People Remember a Shopping App

Every shopping app basically does the same thing — let people buy stuff. What makes an app memorable isn't the "buy" button. It's **the story around why it exists**. Before any marketing, nail a one-line answer to:

> "Why does this exist when Amazon already works fine?"

Airbnb's answer was "live like a local." Etsy's was "handmade, not mass-produced." Depop's was "your closet is your personality." Honey's was "we find the coupon codes so you don't have to."

**For Pick, a possible angle:** *"Stop opening 10 tabs. One search, every store, real prices."* The pitch isn't the tech — it's the feeling of not getting ripped off.

---

## 2. How Other Companies Actually Did It (Real Examples)

### Airbnb — Piggyback on a bigger platform
When Airbnb launched, nobody knew the site existed. So they scraped Craigslist listings and offered Craigslist landlords a free, prettier Airbnb listing. That got their supply side going overnight. They later ran referral programs that paid cash when a friend signed up.

**Lesson for Pick:** Find where your users already hang out (Reddit deal subs, TikTok haul accounts, student discount forums) and meet them there instead of running ads.

### Dollar Shave Club — One video, $0 in ads, 12,000 orders in 48 hours
They filmed a 90-second video where the founder walked through a warehouse saying "Our blades are f***ing great" and cracked jokes. It cost ~$4,500 to make. It went viral because it was honest, funny, and had a clear "why us."

**Lesson for Pick:** A cheap, personality-driven launch video beats a polished corporate ad. Show the frustration (10 browser tabs, fake "lowest price" banners) and then the fix (Pick).

### Etsy — Teach your sellers to sell for you
Etsy wrote a free "Seller Handbook" that taught sellers how to market their shops. Sellers posted their Etsy shops all over Instagram and Pinterest → free traffic for Etsy.

**Lesson for Pick:** Give users a reason to *share* search results. A screenshot that says "I saved $42 on these AirPods using Pick" is free advertising. Build a share button that looks like a brag, not a coupon dump.

### Depop — Be a culture, not a store
Depop didn't market itself as a reselling app. It marketed itself as Instagram-with-a-buy-button — a place where your style lives. They threw "Night Market" pop-up events, collaborated with fashion brands, and let creators become micro-influencers inside the app.

**Lesson for Pick:** The visual identity of a shopping app matters more than the feature list. Amazon looks like a spreadsheet. Pick can look like a feed.

### Honey — The hero is the customer, not the product
Honey's ads showed people saving $5, $10, $20 at checkout with almost no effort. The message was always "you've been paying too much, and you didn't even know."

**Lesson for Pick:** Lead marketing with the savings number, not the feature. "Americans overpay $300/year on online shopping. Pick shows you the cheapest one, every time."

### GoldieBlox / Dollar Shave Club pattern
Both won with one emotional video that got people to say *"finally, someone gets it."*

### DoorDash — Flyers on a campus
Before the app was anywhere, DoorDash printed physical flyers offering $6 delivery and taped them around Stanford. They took orders by hand. Sometimes the lowest-tech channel is the one that works.

**Lesson for Pick:** If you're a student or local founder, launch locally. QR code flyers in dorms, libraries, coffee shops. "Scan to save on your next Amazon order." Super low cost, super high conversion from the right audience.

## 3. How to Stand Out from Amazon and Every Other Shopping App

Amazon wins on three things: selection, speed, and trust. You do **not** beat Amazon at those. You beat Amazon by being better at something Amazon deliberately ignores. Here are the lanes new shopping apps are using:

### Lane A — Be the Honest Friend
Amazon shows you Amazon. Pick shows you every store and tells you where the deal really is. Positioning: *"Amazon's algorithm works for Amazon. Pick's algorithm works for you."*

### Lane B — Speed & Simplicity
Amazon has 40,000 reviews per product. That's overwhelming. A new app can win by showing only the essentials: price, trust score, 1 honest line of why to buy. (This is why Arc browser beat Chrome with a smaller but cleaner feature set.)

### Lane C — Community / Identity
Depop, Whatnot, Poshmark, TikTok Shop won Gen Z by turning shopping into a social feed. If a 19-year-old opens your app and it feels like scrolling TikTok, they'll stay. If it feels like a Best Buy catalog, they won't.

### Lane D — The "Against the System" Story
Dollar Shave Club's whole pitch was "the razor industry is ripping you off." Pick can do the same: *"Online prices change 10 times a day. Stores hope you don't notice. Pick notices for you."* This gives people a villain, which makes marketing way easier.

### Lane E — Niche Down, Then Widen
eBay stopped trying to be Amazon and leaned into collectors. Etsy stopped trying to be eBay and leaned into handmade. Consider: what's the one shopping category Pick is *unreasonably* good at first? Sneakers? School supplies? Gaming setups? Launch there, dominate, then widen.

## 4. Creative, Low-Cost Pitch Tactics (Stuff You Can Actually Do)

### Guerrilla / Cheap Tactics
Guerrilla marketing returns ~4.5–5x the money you put in, according to industry studies. Ideas:

- **Physical price-tag hack.** Print tiny fake price tags that say "You could have paid $X less with Pick." Stick them in public places (with permission) — libraries, cafés, student lounges.
- **TikTok price-reveal series.** Short videos: "I bought these $90 sneakers. Let's see where I *should* have bought them." Reveal on Pick. Every video is free marketing for 30 seconds of filming.
- **The "10 tabs" meme.** Show a browser with 10 tabs of the same product on different sites. Cut to Pick showing all 10 in one view. This is a visual pitch anyone gets in 2 seconds.
- **Pop-up "Price Audit" booth.** Set up at a campus or farmers' market. "Show us your last online purchase. We'll tell you how much you overpaid." Collect signups on the spot.
- **Discord / Reddit drops.** Find subreddits like r/deals, r/frugal, r/BuyItForLife. Post a free tool link (your search page) — no hype, just "here, try it." Upvotes become distribution.

### Influencer / Creator Tactics
- **Pay 20 micro-creators, not 1 macro.** A TikTok creator with 30k followers usually outperforms one with 3M for conversion. Budget-wise: $50–$200 each vs. one $20k deal.
- **Student ambassadors.** Give college students free premium + $1 per signup they drive. Cheap, loyal, and the demographic you want.
- **The "I tried Pick for 30 days" format.** Works on YouTube because it's a story, not an ad.

### Referral / Viral Loops
Airbnb's playbook: both sides get something.
- New user signs up → gets $2 off their next purchase-tracked deal.
- Friend who referred → gets 1 month free premium.
This turns every happy user into a free salesperson.

### PR & Story Hooks
Reporters cover stories, not features. Give them a story:
- "Average American overpays $300/yr shopping online — this 19-year-old built an app to fix it."
- "We tracked Amazon prices for 100 products for 30 days. Here's what we found." Publish the data as a free report; reporters will pick it up.

### The "Free Public Tool" Move
Build a dead-simple free tool on your site: "Paste any Amazon link → see if it's cheaper elsewhere." No login required. It becomes a backlink magnet and gets shared on Twitter/X like crazy. (This is how companies like HubSpot, Ahrefs, and NerdWallet got millions of visitors to free calculators.)

## 5. How to Pitch Pick (Investors, Friends, or Users)

Borrow the structure that worked for Airbnb and Tinder's pitch decks:

1. **Problem** — Online shopping is opaque. Prices change constantly. Amazon hides better deals. *Relatable, 1 sentence.*
2. **Why now** — Price volatility is at an all-time high (real stat to cite). AI makes price scraping cheap. *Trend + timing.*
3. **Solution** — Pick: one search, every retailer, honest prices. *Show the app, don't describe it.*
4. **Why you** — You're the user you're building for. (Founder story matters.)
5. **Traction** — Any number: signups, searches run, money saved across users. Pick the most flattering true metric.
6. **Model** — Freemium, $4.99/mo. Clear, people get it immediately.
7. **The ask** — What you want: users, money, press, partners.

**Storytelling tip:** Tinder's first deck introduced a fictional character ("Matt") who couldn't get dates. Investors remembered Matt. Pick can introduce "Alex" — college student opening 10 tabs trying to find the cheapest textbook. The whole deck is solving Alex's problem.

## 6. A Differentiation Positioning Map

| Competitor      | Their edge             | Pick's counter-angle                             |
|-----------------|------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Amazon          | Selection + speed      | Honesty — we show you when Amazon isn't cheapest |
| Shopify stores  | Brand experience       | Aggregation — one search across all of them      |
| Honey           | Coupon codes           | Total cost + cross-retailer price, not just discounts |
| Rakuten         | Cashback               | Upfront savings, no activation needed            |
| Capital One Shopping | Price history     | More retailers + AI-curated recommendations      |
| Depop / Whatnot | Community & culture    | Utility + savings (different use case — doesn't compete head-on) |
| TikTok Shop     | Discovery + impulse    | Intent-driven — when people know what they want  |

The takeaway: Pick doesn't need to beat any one of these. It needs to be the obvious answer for *"I already know what I want to buy — where do I get it cheapest, fastest, and without getting tricked?"*

---

## 7. Short List: What to Actually Do Next

1. Write one sentence that explains Pick to a stranger in 5 seconds. Test it on 10 people.
2. Pick a lane from Section 3 (Honest Friend, Speed, Community, Against the System, Niche). Commit to one.
3. Make a 60-second launch video in the Dollar Shave Club style. Rough, funny, honest. Don't over-polish.
4. Build the free public "paste-a-link" tool. No signup. Pure backlink + virality bait.
5. Run a local guerrilla campaign — a campus, a neighborhood, a Discord. Small is fine. You want testimonials, not scale, at first.
6. Set up a double-sided referral loop before your first ad dollar.
7. Collect a "money saved" counter — total dollars saved by Pick users. Put it on the homepage. Nothing sells better than a big number going up.
